# Mechanical and simulation model

## Files and data flow

- `src/simulation.ts`: pure TypeScript SI physics, block ownership, transition graph, deterministic integration and accounting. It imports no React or Three.js APIs.
- `src/App.tsx`: controls, validated configuration, dashboard, inventory inspection, event log and SVG power chart. A requestAnimationFrame loop supplies scaled elapsed seconds to the engine; rendering refreshes at approximately 30 fps.
- `src/MineScene.tsx`: one procedural cutaway shaft and one shared headframe enclosing four guided platforms with separate ropes, sheaves and motor-generator/drums. Upper and lower exchange decks have separate rail/trolley lanes and transfer forks for each platform. The view reads engine positions; it never changes physics or ownership.
- `src/simulation.test.ts`: physics, energy, interlocks, transitions, sequential exchanges, inventory, continuity, empty stores, pause/resume/step/reset and frame partitioning tests.

`DispatchSimulator` partitions the configured inventory across exactly four independent `Simulator` lanes (unassigned lanes remain empty when fewer than four blocks exist). Blocks have globally unique IDs and belong to one lane throughout operation. Each lane owns block mass, station/assignment, continuous position, physical height, status and animation phase. Rendering iterates each lane's inventory once; it never clones a load onto another carrier. Each lane has its own upper and lower rails, storage bays and trolleys, separated in the scene. Storage bays flank each rail so trolley movement and transverse pickup do not pass through parked loads. One exchange runs per lane, with at most one load on each platform.

The controller allows at most three loaded hoists to move at once. Every carriage loads and locks its own block, then holds in `ready` with zero conversion and no idle auxiliary load until it receives a dispatch slot. A rotating scan H1 → H2 → H3 → H4 selects the next physically READY carriage; a blocked/unready lane cannot be dispatched. Loading may proceed independently on separate rails. The single shaft has four horizontally separated guideways, independent ropes and motor-generators, and a shared outer excavation/headframe with no internal shaft walls. This is schematic equipment clearance, not evidence that any real shaft is suitable.

Let `L = H / loaded_speed` and `R = H / empty_speed + 8.05 T`. Sustained three-plus-one operation requires `R <= L / 3`, sufficient inventory, and a configured dispatch gap no greater than `L / 3`. The automatic gap is `max(L / 3, (L + R) / 4, configured_minimum_gap)`: it limits conversion to three slots and spreads slower exchanges to avoid unnecessary synchronized stops. No lane skips transfer, empty return or docking to meet the target. With default loaded speed 2 m/s, empty speed 8 m/s, travel 500 m and transfer time 2 s, `L = 250 s`, `R = 78.6 s` and the gap is 83.333 s. Three loaded movements overlap after startup; the fourth completes exchange in its 83.333 s window. Slower return/transfer timings visibly reduce the number of active hoists and power. Startup, inventory exhaustion, manual operation and direction reversal can also interrupt the target.

## Explicit sequence

`waiting → [positioning_empty] → selecting → collecting → transporting → loading → locking → ready → lifting/lowering → unlocking → unloading → delivering → storing → returning_empty → source_docking → selecting/waiting`

The same graph works in reverse for charging. Selecting approaches the source bay; collecting slides the block across a telescopic fork onto the source trolley. Transporting moves the trolley and block together. Loading slides the block from trolley to platform. Ownership passes to the hoist at the end of loading; locking secures the block and retracts the fork. The destination trolley approaches the dock during unlocking; unloading slides the block onto it. Delivering and storing move it to its matching bay. The block is stored before the empty hoist returns.

Each lane's private transition function accepts only graph edges, checks dock height for transfer/locking phases and requires exactly one secured hoist load before vertical travel. Empty travel and source docking require an empty platform. `ready` requires a docked, secured load; only the controller releases it. The dashboard maps granular phases to READY, LOADING, DESCENDING/ASCENDING, BOTTOM_DOCKING, UNLOADING, RETURNING_EMPTY, TOP_DOCKING and WAITING. Public control methods cannot jump to arbitrary phases. Fleet pause freezes every lane and scheduled dispatch. Step advances all lanes on the same clock to the next phase boundary or scheduled dispatch, then pauses. A direction change drains the assigned exchange and empty return on every lane before restarting staggered dispatch in the opposite direction; opposite travel directions never overlap. Repositioning for the new direction is metered as an empty movement. No source inventory means waiting, with no energy consumption. Automatic Exchange OFF drains already assigned blocks and empty returns, then holds; enabling it resumes reloading on available lanes. Pause/Resume remains a separate control.

Integration splits an elapsed-time increment at every lane phase boundary and scheduled start, so all lanes share one clock and large time steps cannot charge travel energy during downtime. Electrical power, energy and auxiliaries are summed across lanes; parallelism changes the power profile, not inventory capacity or block energy. Endpoint tolerances snap interpolation to exact docking coordinates without discontinuous visible movement. `assertInvariants()` checks both lane interlocks and global inventory/ID and energy conservation.

## Energy and power

All accounting uses SI physical height and mass. Potential energy is `m × 9.81 × h` joules; dividing by 3,600,000 yields kWh. For each loaded travel increment, the engine differences actual block heights:

- Descent: released potential increases by `m g |Δh|`; electrical generation increases by released energy × discharge efficiency.
- Ascent: gained potential increases by `m g Δh`; charging electricity increases by gained energy / charging efficiency.
- Instantaneous loaded conversion is `m g v / 1000` kW, multiplied by discharge efficiency for output or divided by charging efficiency for input.
- Transfers and empty travel produce no electricity. Auxiliaries consume `configured kW × phase seconds / 3600`. Net grid power subtracts auxiliary power; net grid energy = generated − charging − auxiliary consumption. Pause has zero power and consumes no energy.

The initial inventory is fully charged. Conservation is checked against released gravitational energy, not previous charging counters. Repeated charge/discharge cycles lose energy. Maximum capacity is total block potential at the upper station; current stored potential includes every partially raised or lowered block. The displayed discharge rating is per hoist at constant configured loaded speed; the chart sums conversion and auxiliary import across all lanes.

A fleet block cycle has a loaded traversal, a separately configured empty traversal, seven full-duration transfer phases and three 0.35-duration locking/docking phases: `H/v_loaded + H/v_empty + 8.05 T`, before any READY waiting. Last measured cycle durations include READY waiting. The single-lane test model can run without the fleet gate/source-docking phase; its base cycle uses `7.7 T`. Round-trip estimate over a full inventory is `(capacity × η_discharge − auxiliary_discharge) / (capacity / η_charge + auxiliary_charge)`, with identical base auxiliary estimates in each direction. It excludes the possible one-off initial reposition. Actual electricity counters include every executed phase. Negative estimated net output is displayed as zero useful efficiency.

## Reeving and auxiliary model

The 2:1 illustration has a fixed upper anchor, two rope legs around a platform-mounted moving sheave, and an upper deflection sheave leading to the drum. 1:1 removes the moving sheave and uses one load-bearing leg. Ideal block-only rope tension is `m g / n`; rope speed and travel are `n` times platform speed and travel. The visual sheave/drum angles derive from rope travel. **Mechanical advantage changes force and travel, not total energy.** The option does not change conversion efficiency.

Transfer auxiliaries are a constant configurable load across selecting, collecting, transporting, loading, locking, unlocking, unloading, delivering, storing and source docking. Empty hoist travel has a separate constant electrical load in both directions, conservatively with no regeneration. These surrogate loads cover controls, trolley/fork drives and empty platform handling; they do not independently solve motor torque, acceleration, platform potential energy, cable mass or friction. No idle standing load is modelled. Horizontal transfers leave the block's main gravitational height unchanged. Linear station motion is a schematic kinematic cycle, not a rated drive-speed or collision-clearance design.

## Visual and site limitations

The 500 m default physical travel is rendered as 30 scene units. The scene uses schematic horizontal dimensions and block envelopes for legibility, not physically sized 20-tonne solid geometry. Guideways are 6.5 scene units apart along world X within a single shaft envelope, so the four loads appear horizontally side by side; station transverse positions are rendered at 0.7 times their simulation coordinates to fit the adjacent exchange lanes, consistently for blocks, bays and forks. This continuous mapping does not change physical heights, sequencing or energy. There are no internal shaft walls or separate headframes. Outside walls remain transparent, with an open front cutaway and lower transfer openings. No external models, textures or fonts are fetched. Camera controls support orbit, pan, zoom and four presets framing the common shaft and exchange stations. A browser without WebGL retains simulation controls with a fallback message.

This is not a validated mine design and does not establish suitability or authorization for a real mine in Valea Jiului. Shaft diameter, usable depth, ground stability, hoisting loads, storage space, ventilation, mine safety and access are all unknown. Further study would also need acceleration/braking profiles, steelwork and foundations, rope and drum sizing, redundant safety systems, thermal limits, equipment efficiencies across operating points, control failure modes and grid constraints.
